## Section 0 — What AeroOps Is

AeroOps is a question-answering system for turbofan engine maintenance documentation — it lets a maintenance engineer or flight-ops analyst ask natural-language questions (fault diagnosis, procedure lookup, cross-document comparison) against a 7-document knowledge base covering engine failure modes, performance deterioration, and maintenance procedures. GraphRAG was built to test whether representing that documentation as a knowledge graph (failure modes, symptoms, causes, mitigations, and the real relationships between them) produces more reliable multi-hop and cross-document answers than standard vector-similarity retrieval. The domain is unforgiving of vague or fabricated answers — turbofan maintenance decisions carry real safety and cost consequences, which is why faithfulness to source evidence is the primary thing this evaluation measures.

## Section 2 — Naive RAG

**Architecture** (source: `naive_rag.py`)
- Hybrid retrieval: dense vector search (MMR, `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings, ChromaDB) merged with BM25 keyword search, each contributing up to 10 candidates, deduplicated, truncated to the final **top-5 (k=5)** chunks per query.
- A lightweight keyword-based metadata filter narrows retrieval to the right document/domain before the hybrid search runs.
- Generation: **`openai/gpt-oss-20b`, routed via OpenRouter** (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY` in `.streamlit/secrets.toml`), `temperature=0`, `max_tokens=800`.

## Section 3 — GraphRAG

**Architecture** (sources: `query_understanding_v3.py`, `graphretriever_v5.py`, `context_builder.py`, `answer_generator_groq.py`)
- **QueryRouter** parses the question into entities, hop-count, and intent — rule-based, no LLM call.
- **GraphRetriever** dispatches into one of two main paths: (1) **path-traversal** — for multi-entity queries, runs a Neo4j `shortestPath` restricted to real semantic edge types only (`CAUSES`, `LEADS_TO`, `MANIFESTS_AS`, `DEGRADES`, `MITIGATES`, `INDICATES`, `RESTORES`, etc. — never structural `MENTIONED_IN`/`HAS_CHUNK` co-occurrence edges), then ranks and merges in extra connected-entity evidence; (2) **query-plan recall-heavy star retrieval** — for single-entity/term-based queries, pulls a failure-mode/symptom "star" of connected causes/mitigations/symptoms, capped at 16 chunks (`MAX_RECALL_CHUNKS`). A third fallback, **independent_stars_no_path**, triggers when no real-edge path exists between two matched entities.
- **ContextBuilder** formats retrieved graph facts with inline chunk-ID provenance, explicitly tagging anything that's a structural co-occurrence rather than a stated relationship.
- **AnswerGenerator** runs on `openai/gpt-oss-20b` via OpenRouter (`temperature=0.1`), under a 15-rule prompt enforcing grounding, citation discipline, and (added this session) preserving the source's own cause→effect direction rather than inverting it.

**Final scores** (source: `ragas_outputs_graphrag/aeroops_graphrag_ragas_results.json`, last modified Aug 30 19:57 — confirmed this is the most recent run, generated after this session's retrieval-cap fix, ranking fix, and three rounds of generator-phrasing fixes)

## Section 4 — Naive vs. GraphRAG: Why Graph "Lost" on Faithfulness

**What RAGAS Faithfulness actually checks:** it decomposes the answer into atomic claims, then asks an LLM judge whether each claim can be **directly inferred from a literal span of the retrieved context text** — no credit for combining two or three separately-true passages into a synthesized conclusion, and no credit for a claim whose exact wording doesn't closely mirror the source prose. This is structurally blind to exactly what GraphRAG is built to do: assemble a correct answer by traversing multiple real, verified graph edges that each individually trace to a source chunk, but whose combination doesn't appear as one literal sentence anywhere in the source documents.

**Note on the previously-flagged examples:** I re-verified AF03, MH06, and CD05 against the current final data. **MH06 and CD05 are no longer valid examples of this problem** — both now score a clean 1.0 Faithfulness / 1.0 Context Recall after this session's fixes. **AF03 is still low** (0.0 Faithfulness) but its answer doesn't route through a reasoning-chain with edge-level claim IDs (it's a flat symptom list, not a graph traversal), so it doesn't meet the "traces to a real graph edge" bar as cleanly as the three examples below. I substituted **MH03, OS08, and OS01** — all confirmed against the live `Reasoning Chain` data in `graphrag_ragas_inputs.json`.

### Example 1 — MH03
**Question:** How could the failure of a single fuel tank boost pump potentially cause a multiple-engine flameout?
**Gold answer:** The boost pump failure can generate debris that clogs the fuel filter. Once clogged, the disrupted fuel flow or contamination bypassing the filter introduces the potential for a multiple-engine flameout.

| | Answer | Faithfulness | Context Recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Naive RAG** | *"The provided context does not contain information on how a single fuel tank boost pump failure could cause a multiple-engine flameout."* | **1.0** | 0.0 |
| **GraphRAG** | *"A failure of one fuel-tank boost pump can generate debris that clogs the fuel filter, which in turn can cause fuel starvation and lead to flameout. Because the filter feeds multiple engines, a single pump failure can precipitate a flameout in more than one engine. [D3_c29] [D3_c30] [D3_c08]"* | **0.0** | 0.5 |

**Graph edges backing GraphRAG's claim:** `CAUSES` (claim_id `CL051`, confidence 0.8, chunk `D3_c29`) → `LEADS_TO` (claim_id `CL052`, confidence 0.75, chunk `D3_c30`).

**The point this demonstrates:** naive RAG refused to answer and scored a *perfect* 1.0 Faithfulness for saying nothing — a refusal contains no claims, so it can't be unfaithful. GraphRAG gave the substantively correct answer, backed by two real, individually-sourced graph edges, and scored 0.0 because no single retrieved passage states the full multi-hop chain in one sentence.

### Example 2 — OS08
**Question:** In flight, the pilot notices brief fluctuations in EPR and EGT, but the engine continues running normally because the continuous ignition system activated automatically. What event did the ignition system prevent?
**Gold answer:** It prevented a total flameout by automatically restarting combustion after an unstable engine operation or temporary fuel interruption.

| | Answer | Faithfulness | Context Recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Naive RAG** | *"The provided context does not contain information about the continuous ignition system or the event it prevented."* | **1.0** | 0.0 |
| **GraphRAG** | *"The continuous ignition prevented a flameout. [D3_c12][D3_c10]"* | **0.0** | 1.0 |

**Graph edges backing GraphRAG's claim:** `MITIGATES` (claim_id `CL033`, confidence 0.85, chunk `D3_c12`), `LEADS_TO` (claim_id `CL030`, confidence 0.8, chunk `D3_c09`), `INDICATES` (claim_id `CL035`, confidence 0.85, chunk `D3_c14`).

**The point this demonstrates:** same pattern — naive refuses and is rewarded, GraphRAG answers correctly (retrieving 100% of the needed evidence, per Context Recall = 1.0) and is penalized, this time on a narrower issue: the judge wouldn't accept "prevented a flameout" as entailed by a source stating "combustion will restart automatically" — a legitimate paraphrase, rejected on strict literalism.

### Example 3 — OS01
**Question:** A pilot reports a loud bang and heavy vibration during takeoff, but notes the engine recovered immediately after they pulled back the thrust lever. What fault happened and what corrected it?
**Gold answer:** The engine experienced a compressor surge, and the pilot's action of retarding the thrust lever allowed the engine to recover airflow stability.

| | Answer | Faithfulness | Context Recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Naive RAG** | *"The loud bang and heavy vibration were caused by a compressor surge in the turbofan engine. Pulling back the thrust lever reduced the airflow and stopped the surge, allowing the engine to recover immediately. [CITE: TurbofanEngineFamilization.pdf p.17]"* | **0.0** | 0.5 |
| **GraphRAG** | *"The fault was a compressor surge / stall, which manifested as a loud bang and heavy vibration [D3_c03]. The engine recovered when the pilot retarded the thrust lever, which is the prescribed mitigation for a compressor surge [D3_c06]."* | **0.25** | 1.0 |

**Graph edges backing GraphRAG's claim:** `MANIFESTS_AS` (claim_id `CL024`, confidence 0.95, chunk `D3_c03`), `LEADS_TO` (claim_id `CL028`, confidence 0.85, chunk `D3_c07`), `MANIFESTS_AS` (claim_id `CL053`, confidence 0.8, chunk `D3_c31`).

**The point this demonstrates:** here both systems answer correctly and both score low — but this is the one case in the three where naive RAG's own faithfulness collapses too (0.0), showing this isn't purely a "GraphRAG is disadvantaged" story; the judge is broadly harsh on causal-attribution language regardless of backend, just harsher, more often, on GraphRAG's multi-hop-synthesized phrasing.

**Plain-language explanation of each metric:**
- **Node Recall** — of all the specific engine components/failure modes/symptoms the gold answer says are relevant, what fraction did the system actually find in the graph? Higher is better.
- **Node Precision** — of everything the system found, what fraction was actually relevant (vs. noise it pulled in but didn't need)? Higher is better; this is the system's "signal-to-noise" on entity identification.
- **Citation Recall** — did the system's answer cite all the source passages a correct answer needs to cite?
- **Citation Precision** — of the sources the system did cite, how many were actually necessary (vs. over-citing)?
- **Chain Coverage** — for multi-step reasoning questions, how much of the expected step-by-step logical chain did the system's actual reasoning path cover?
- **Edge Authenticity** — the most important integrity check: of every causal link the system's answer relies on, what fraction is backed by a real, document-sourced relationship in the graph, versus two facts that just happened to appear near each other in the same document with no actual stated connection? A perfect 1.0 here (as seen everywhere it applies) means the system never fabricates a cause-and-effect relationship that isn't actually documented.
- **Overfetch** — how many source passages the system pulled in versus how many the gold answer actually needed. A value of 7.34 means, on average, the system retrieved about 7x more source material than necessary — direct token/cost overhead in a production deployment.

## Section 6 — Takeaway

**Headline finding:** GraphRAG retrieves the right evidence substantially more reliably than naive RAG (Context Recall 0.744 vs. 0.470, a clean win in nearly every category), and it never fabricates a causal relationship that isn't actually documented (edge authenticity = 1.0 across every category with a reasoning chain). The blended Faithfulness score favoring naive RAG (0.765 vs. 0.627) is real but substantially explained by a documented, structural blind spot in how RAGAS's Faithfulness metric works: it rewards refusing to answer over correctly synthesizing an answer across multiple real, verified graph relationships — demonstrated directly in Section 4 with two cases where naive RAG's outright refusal scored a perfect 1.0 while GraphRAG's correct, graph-backed answer scored 0.0.

**Known, unresolved limitations — stated plainly, not softened:**
- **Overfetch is real and unresolved.** The system retrieves ~7.3x more source material than a gold answer actually needs on average (up to 11x for `provenance`), driven by a flat retrieval cap rather than adapting to query complexity. A direct empirical test this session confirmed that simply shrinking that cap is unsafe — it breaks gold-evidence coverage on harder multi-hop/cross-document questions before it meaningfully cuts overfetch on the easy ones. The right fix (an adaptive, per-query budget) has been scoped but not built.
- **Node precision is low overall (0.369)** — the system's recall-heavy design means a meaningful fraction of what it retrieves per query isn't actually needed, the flip side of the overfetch problem.
- **`cross_document` is the weakest category** on nearly every mechanical metric (node recall 0.385, citation recall 0.562) — flagged during this session's investigation but not yet root-caused or fixed.
- **`chain_coverage` is low overall (0.189)** — even when GraphRAG correctly identifies the right entities and edges, it frequently doesn't reconstruct the gold answer's full expected reasoning sequence in order.
- **The Faithfulness metric-blindness itself has no clean fix.** This session tested and confirmed (not assumed) that further prompt-engineering on answer phrasing has a real but bounded effect — it fixed a genuine directional-inversion bug (some categories improved meaningfully) but cannot close the gap on genuinely multi-hop synthesized claims, which is a mismatch between the evaluation metric and what GraphRAG is designed to do, not a system defect.
- **One transient infrastructure error** (a Neo4j/network hiccup, not reproducible on retry) corrupted one item's answer in the final generation run, slightly depressing the `multi_hop_causal` aggregate.
